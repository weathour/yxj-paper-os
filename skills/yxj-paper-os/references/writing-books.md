## Problem and contribution

Start from the scientific understanding the article offers its readers: the question,
the author's idea and the scholarly increment. For the creative work of choosing that
intellectual form, use [conceptual framing](conceptual-framing.md). C develops the
problem and warrant, T the scholarly response, W the selection and story, and S the
reader-facing explanation. Tensions and trade-offs can clarify a particular relation;
constructions, synthesis and new representations can organize the work in their own
terms. The linked published cases show how these choices change the article.

For first-encounter design, use the [layered question and immediate contribution example](question-material-structure.md#q0-先让期刊读者看见问题增量与所得).
Make a disciplinary concern, the
unresolved judgment and the paper's advance quickly intelligible, and give the reader
an early substantive insight. This practice draws on the audience, significance,
story and information-flow methods below.

**When a draft names a topic or technique but leaves the question unclear**, distinguish
the object being studied, the relation that needs understanding, and what the intended
reader gains from its answer. A temporary “studying X / finding out Y / understanding Z”
sentence can expose the missing connection. It is a thinking tool, not required final
prose or proof that Z matters. Early exploratory work may not yet resolve the third part.
Look for change, composition, classification, a boundary, a counterexample, a source
discrepancy or an unexplained connection; establish that the available work can answer
the selected question. C3–4; S7; W6–7.

**When significance sounds generic**, connect the small question to a specific larger
understanding or supported use. A conceptual cost is an important question remaining
unanswered; it need not be a social harm. Separate who needs the result from what changes
for them. Do not manufacture consensus, a literature gap, priority or an application.
Replication, local information, negative results and a new explanation may have value
under their own reader and contribution contracts. C4; T7; W18–19.

**When results are a list**, determine the relationship, explanation, comparison,
bounded capability or synthesis worth making this article about. Select the material
that establishes and explains that answer before giving every existing result a role.
A broad question that merely accommodates the whole inventory does not resolve the
choice. Distinguish a research question (`to learn`) from an objective (`to do`), and
its supported answer from its scholarly increment. A familiar relation can orient the
reader without becoming a novelty claim. Mathematical characterization, method
construction and exploratory description need not invent a testable hypothesis.
W2,6–9; C3–4,8.

To choose among plausible centers, compare the actual answers and their support, not
just alternative titles. Consider which consequential relationship each makes clearer,
what scholarly difference it establishes, and what material it would require readers
to learn. Let evidence and author scope constrain the choice. A memorable example may
orient the article while a less dramatic quantitative result carries its contribution;
parallel answers may legitimately remain parallel. This is an operational synthesis
of the question, significance and story methods, not a quotation or a rule that one
book prescribes a universal selection formula.

## Argument and support

**When a conclusion has citations or data but the inference is unclear**, recover the
claim, reasons, support and the rule that makes those reasons bear on this claim
(`warrant`). Check whether the rule is reasonable, bounded, appropriate to this domain,
consistent with relevant competing principles, and covers this instance. Evidence
reliability and relevance are different problems. State the connection when readers
cannot safely recover it or a material challenge requires it; familiar reasoning does
not need a paragraph announcing every premise. C7–11; T4,6,17–18.

Match the support obligation to the claim. Definitions, assumptions and deduction can
establish a mathematical result; simulations may illustrate or test a prediction;
observations, comparisons and causal identification have different jobs. An illustrative
case is not a universal proof. Numbers require the actual quantity, units, population or
denominator, comparison and uncertainty meaning. Writing guidance cannot supply a
missing proof, control, identification argument or experiment. C8–9; T17–18; W8.

**When a substantive objection survives**, fairly specify the challenged premise,
evidence, inference or scope and answer that point with support. A strong objection may
require a narrower claim, a different explanation or abandoning the claim. A concession
cannot repair a broken theorem. Use actual alternatives or explicitly hypothetical
questions; do not invent group beliefs, weak opponents or a compulsory “balanced” view.
Do not add imagined-reviewer rebuttals where the actual science and explanation suffice.
C10–11; T6,11; W18.

## Academic dialogue and sources

**When related work reads as a list**, organize sources by the current question and the
relationship they establish: support, disagreement, complementary scope, method, limit
or explanation. First reconstruct each important source fairly, then select what this
paper needs. Identify the part accepted, changed or extended and the reason. Agreement
can add evidence, a consequence, an explanation or a new setting; disagreement alone
does not establish novelty. C5–6; T1–4,14–15,18; W6.

Keep source judgments, the current author's inference and shared background identifiable
through the passage. A signal verb names what a source actually does; it cannot turn
“suggests” into “proves.” Source chaining and lateral reading can guide a bounded search
when that search is requested or needed, but citation counts and publication prestige
do not establish reliability. Implicit dialogue reconstructed by the reader remains an
inference, not a quotation or documented consensus. T5,14–15; C5–6.

**For an important quotation**, identify whose words they are, retain their actual
meaning and conditions, and explain why this wording bears on the present argument.
The amount of framing depends on the passage; a short familiar quotation need not
receive three formulaic sentences. Quotation, detailed paraphrase and concise summary
serve different purposes. A paraphrase reorganizes the idea in the writer's words and
still attributes it; word substitution is not sufficient. The quote proves those words
were written, not that the underlying claim is true. T2–3; C14; S Appendix II.

## Scientific story and unit structure

**For drafting or global redesign, selection is part of composition.** Recover the
scientific answer from the evidence and its relation to existing knowledge; then choose
the reader's route to understanding and judging it. Research effort, technical rigor
and independent usefulness do not determine an item's prominence. Compare what each
candidate item contributes with the additional concepts it requires. Choose among full
explanation, a short statement, a reference, supporting placement and omission. Preserve
support for retained claims and material adverse evidence; original research records
remain intact. W2,8,16; C12; S8. The [selection case](composition-examples.md#selection-from-completed-work-to-an-articles-answer)
shows the distinction between safeguarding science and retaining every branch in prose.
Test a proposed omission by identifying the understanding or evidential support it would
remove. Then compare full explanation, a short account and supporting placement. The
[model-and-derivation example](composition-examples.md#when-the-model-and-derivation-carry-the-explanation)
shows why the same selection rule can require more technical explanation in another task.

**When readers cannot see why the work proceeds this way**, identify orientation,
challenge, the work answering it, and the understanding it yields. W's OCAR describes
these functions; IMRaD names sections. ABDCE, lead-first and result-led orders are
alternatives suited to the audience and material, not a recipe for every paper. Organize
from the evidence, then present from the reader's needs. Announce the main result early
enough to orient the reader; avoid invented suspense or a hero's discovery diary.
W2,4–10; C12,16; T17; S7–8.

For sustained reading, use [reading milestones](paragraph-rhythm-closure.md#p0-阅读里程碑把章节推进落实为理解所得)
to specify what readers can now explain or judge, the grounds supplied, and how that
understanding supports the next question. Make these gains locatable through headings,
core sentences, displays and interpretations so selective readers can recover the main
argument and find where to examine it. This is an operational synthesis from the
author's writing practice, drawing on the unit, argument and information-flow methods
above; the term is not attributed to a book. Milestones guide emphasis and entry order,
with no fixed number, separate required record or claim of measured reader satisfaction.

Match the opening's promise to the actual closing. Start at a recognizable object,
problem or relation; provide only the background that connects it to the question.
“Little is known” does not define a gap without its known boundaries and importance.
The conclusion synthesizes the supported answer and its meaning; future work is a
specific remaining question, not invented impact. Abstracts and titles make a compact
accurate promise; their information order depends on article type and reader knowledge.
C13,16; W5–9; T7,10; S7.

**When a section or paragraph lacks a point**, recover its actual job and central
relation. Point-first suits quick orientation; point-last can build an inference whose
premises must come first. Both need a recognizable point and thematic continuity.
Definitions, procedures, proofs, transitions and reproducibility details can do necessary
work without a new headline claim. Decide which units the article needs before assigning
their jobs. Order retained units by real dependency, comparison, familiarity or explanatory
purpose; do not turn page order into scientific implication.
W10–11; C12–13; S8; T8,10. See [composition cases](composition-examples.md).

## Language and information flow

For sentences, paragraphs, wording, punctuation and bilingual realization, use
[authorial-style.md](authorial-style.md). Its conditional operations implement S3–11,
C17, W12–16 and T5,8–10: meaningful subjects and actions, stable referents, old-to-new
links, deliberate stress, useful nominalizations, justified voice, clear long sentences
and enough connective explanation. Check the paragraph before improving one sentence
at the expense of its neighbors. Preserve definitions and qualifications, not merely
unchanged symbols. An appropriate passage can stay unchanged.

## Revision and practice

**When polishing does not resolve the problem**, locate it in the current text:
structure/meaning, clarity, flow or language. W17's SCFL suggests a useful priority,
not exactly four passes; a wording correction may reveal a structural or scientific
question. T11 adds return-to-source, example-versus-claim, comparison-baseline and
unintended-implication checks. C13/S's preface likewise make revision recursive.

For global work, reverse-outline what the draft actually claims and supplies; compare
the opening, support and closing. For local work, read the whole changed passage with
neighbors and check changed referents, evidence strength and implications. Shortening
first compares functions and selects dispensable material, then trims wording. Preserve
prerequisites, explanation, source credit and material adverse evidence. Some revisions
need more words or restore a previous version for the new context. C12–14; W16–18; T11.

Use entry order, explanatory space, visual emphasis and closure to check the actual
hierarchy. A contribution list, added transitions or an overview cannot compensate for
secondary content occupying the principal explanatory space. A core sentence should
give a result or relationship when that is the unit's point; an announcement of what
the section studies supplies navigation only. C13; S8; W10,16.

To learn a method, use a complete input, one realized output, the reason for its choices
and the meaning it must preserve. For English paragraph study, begin with a complete
published original and its context; distinguish source wording from an analyst's
comparison or a newly proposed rewrite. Compare the original book's examples and
alternatives when needed. A focused exercise or reading aloud can expose a concrete
problem. C teachers' postscript; W1,17,21; T11; S preface/exercises/answers.

## Displays, audiences and ethics

Use [display-workflow.md](display-workflow.md) for a display's scientific and editorial
role, and thesis-figure-skill for concrete figure design and production.
C15 adds choosing the representation by its question: exact lookup,
discrete comparison, continuous trend, composition or relationship. Explain units,
uncertainty, aggregation and comparison conditions; axes and grouping can change the
apparent inference.

Use [reader knowledge and evaluation tasks](question-material-structure.md#reader-tasks)
for the actual reader, and [venue calibration](venue-calibration.md) for article type
and applicable requirements. W19–20/T9,12–13/S1–2,12/C2 guide how much shared knowledge to
assume and how to explain without condescension. A message box can help broad-audience
communication: actual audience/issue, problem, why it matters, supported solution and
benefit. Current venue conventions enter through venue calibration; Chinese-English
choices enter through authorial style.

Keep necessary complexity where the reader must understand it. Some book examples contain
incorrect scientific interpretations or change meaning during revision: notably W8's
p-value interpretations, T's technical/public-policy cases, and S's threshold/answer
changes. Adopt their writing operation only after checking the actual project's meaning.
See the source analyses for exact exceptions and source-internal discrepancies.

to its book and chapter.

For English literal text, native TOC or an exact EPUB ref:

```bash
python3 /home/weathour/文档/CPS-Papers/shared/paper-writing-tools-lab/scripts/query_academic_writing_books.py --book craft --query 'a warrant is' --limit 1
python3 /home/weathour/文档/CPS-Papers/shared/paper-writing-tools-lab/scripts/query_academic_writing_books.py --book science --toc 'Story Structure' --limit 1

```

Each lookup selects one book, verifies carrier and reader hashes, and checks returned
text against the original XHTML. Inspect `heading_path` and `element`: literal hits may
be navigation or headings. Follow the substantive ref and surrounding argument, example
and exception. Results cap matches/context; long text is explicitly incomplete and can
continue with `--ref REF --offset NEXT_OFFSET`. Do not silently quote a truncated block.
Image refs return original member/hash; extract that exact member to a temporary file
and view it when the illustration carries the needed content.

Whitespace is normalized. `inline_annotations` exposes deletion/insertion and raised
text; verify original markup for quotations, edits, labels, tables and mathematical
notation. A deletion example may contain both old and replacement words in flat text.
State source ID, carrier hash, member/fragment and block ref; these are local locators,
not printed page numbers. Cite the registered book/edition when attribution is needed;
do not insert writing textbooks into a manuscript bibliography by default.

`not_found`, a changed hash, a wrong-book ref or a carrier gap is a bounded failure.
Refine the term within the same selected book or follow its native chapter/analysis;
report unavailable wording rather than inventing it or sweeping the raw library. T's
introductory empty dialogue labels and S's misused image do not supply missing quotes.
If this local reader is unavailable elsewhere, report that limit and use an explicitly
provided carrier/locator; ordinary writing can still use these conditional methods.
