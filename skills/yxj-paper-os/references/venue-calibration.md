# Matched-Venue Writing Calibration

Read this only when target-venue wording, sentence patterns, or organization materially
affects the current decision. Exemplars calibrate how justified science is presented;
they never decide what the paper may claim.

## Build the smallest useful cohort

Prefer repository-declared or author-supplied papers. Otherwise search only when the
requested calibration requires it. Match, in order, the same journal, article type or
section, publication era, same or closely related topic, and the same manuscript locus
and communicative job. Verify source identity from publisher metadata or the article.
State every relaxed match. One paper is an example, not a venue convention.

Stop once the cohort resolves the actual wording or organization decision. Report a
recurring pattern as an observed count over the inspected cohort with precise locators;
never turn descriptive counts into quality scores or build a general style model.

## Compare like with like

Compare abstract gap sentences with abstract gap sentences, contribution statements
with contribution statements, quantitative result sentences with comparable results,
mechanism explanations with mechanism explanations, limitations with limitations, and
captions with the same display type. Whole-paper averages for sentence length, passive
voice, or transitions do not decide whether a particular sentence works.

Inspect only features relevant to the decision:

- **wording:** field terms, compounds and hyphenation, spelling, abbreviations,
  assertion and hedging verbs, transitions, and avoided internal or process diction;
- **sentences:** the reader job, information focus, subject and agency, tense and voice,
  length and rhythm, parallelism, and placement of claim, evidence, and caveat; and
- **organization:** titles, abstracts, contribution statements, section openings and
  closings, result--discussion flow, captions, tables, citations, and limitations.

## Turn observations into bounded decisions

- **Adopt** stable terminology, spelling, hyphenation, abbreviation, and format norms.
- **Adapt** recurring rhetorical choices whose fit depends on this paper's evidence,
  reader path, and author voice.
- **Avoid** one paper's distinctive wording, unsupported claim strength, and formulaic
  language that performs no inferential job.

For a material calibration, keep a temporary decision record:

```text
manuscript locus and sentence job:
current defect:
cohort observation, count, and locators:
adopt / adapt / avoid decision:
scientific semantic invariants:
applied change:
post-edit check:
```

Semantic invariants include the actor, object, scope, quantifiers, comparison baseline,
direction and magnitude, uncertainty, causal strength, and evidence locator. Apply a
supported decision only to the affected text, then check the changed passage and every
dependent abstract, body, caption, table, conclusion, term, and translation surface.
Any change to an invariant reopens the local evidence and inference check.

Preserve scientific meaning, citations, settled architecture, and author voice. Do not
copy distinctive prose, transfer claims, or force conformity. Persist a venue decision
only when it changed the paper and cannot be recovered safely; do not create a template
dossier or style database.
