# Published English paragraphs: choosing syntax and words in context

Use this reference for substantive English drafting, paragraph revision, or a question about a particular construction or word. Start with the passage's scientific object, evidence and job; then select the relevant comparison below. Read one suitable case and its complete original paragraph, extending to a contrasting case when it changes the decision. Do not load the whole collection for a local edit.

## Paragraph development

Follow the current scientific claim into the paragraph's actual job: it may separate two
judgments, explain a coexistence, justify a construction, delimit a guarantee or move
to a task with different conditions. Choose the subject, information order and endpoint
that carry this relationship. A definition or routine method unit can support it without
staging a new conflict.

Compare a task–gap–answer paragraph, a mechanism that makes a method limitation intelligible, and an explanation following a formula. Their different jobs require different amounts of background and different endpoints. Do not impose one paragraph template on all three.

## Transitions

Read both sides of the transition. A change from an idealized population to a plausible selection mechanism, from measured behavior to an inferred consequence, and from external observations to a proposed mechanism each requires a different bridge. A connective cannot supply a missing reason or turn reading order into mathematical dependence.

## Closure

Close the actual unit of explanation. An open performance guarantee, a review’s proposed research agenda, and an unquantified tolerance are different endpoints. Keep a forecast or recommendation distinct from a completed result. Retain what was established while making the outstanding object precise; do not automatically append a broad promise or a future-work list.

## Subjects and actions

Choose the subject according to the relation being explained: a paper part can announce a task; a planner or controller can perform a physical operation; a matrix can carry structural information. Do not force a technical representation into causal agency, or turn every sentence into an author action.

## Voice

Passive voice can keep the affected object or available guarantee in focus; active author subjects can make a modeling choice or interpretation accountable. Check whether the actor matters and whether the reader can recover it. When revising for an adopted quantitative threshold, choose voice changes that also serve the passage and preserve its conditions and scientific meaning.

## Information order

Preserve the reader's known object while introducing the new relation. Introduce an unfamiliar mathematical object before asking the reader to interpret its role; place a decisive condition where it governs the claim being read. Given-to-new progression is a useful explanation of a choice, not a license to move every condition to the end.

## Long sentences and punctuation

Ask what a long sentence holds together. Parentheses, dashes, a colon or a semicolon can distinguish scope, explanation or related propositions; none repairs an unclear relation. When splitting, preserve quantifier scope, the comparison dimension and the referent of each continuation. When retaining length, ensure the main clause remains recoverable.

## Verb choice

Inspect the subject, object, evidence and scope of the verb together. Establishing existence, identifying a measured feature, interpreting a pattern and extending an implication are different acts. There is no context-free ladder that orders every use of show, demonstrate, suggest and prove; narrowing the object can matter more than changing the verb.

## Disciplinary collocations

Keep a term's field-specific object and established relation. A state-space realization is not hardware deployment; instability frequency and degree are different quantities; a direct sum and a block-diagonal arrangement describe related but distinct things. Technical repetition is often necessary for precision.

## Qualifiers

Identify what each qualifier restricts: the mathematical domain, averaging operation, observational support, practical frequency, knowledge of the literature, or proposed external relevance. Preserve decisive restrictions next to their claim. Do not spread vague hedges across an exact result or turn a scoped observation into a universal one.

## Near synonyms

Compare candidate expressions in a complete proposition. Ask whether the replacement changes the object, causal commitment, evidence type, quantifier or knowledge state. Prefer the accurate term over stylistic variation. A comparison supplied by the analyst is an explanation of a choice, not another quotation from the source.
